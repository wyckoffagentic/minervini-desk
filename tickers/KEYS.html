1. Business model and revenue mix

Electronic design, emulation and test: instruments (oscilloscopes, network/signal analysers), network and protocol emulation, EDA/simulation software and services. Customers use it to design and validate wireless and wireline communications gear, AI data-center interconnects (800G/1.6T optics), semiconductors, automotive/EV electronics, and aerospace/defense systems. Revenue is mostly hardware plus software, sold per project/capex cycle, with a growing software/services element. The business is order-driven, so orders and book-to-bill are the lead indicators.

6. Competitive position / moat

  • Keysight is the largest pure-play electronic test and measurement company. It tends to be first to market on new standards (5G/6G, 1.6T/3.2T optics, NTN satellite direct-to-device, PCIe/UALink-class interconnects), so it gets designed in early in R&D cycles and then sells manufacturing test.
  • The Spirent deal deepened network/protocol emulation (Ethernet and AI-fabric load testing, wireless emulation), a direct fit for AI data-center buildouts. Commercial Communications grew +56% in Q3.
  • It holds 113 validated NR-NTN conformance test cases (GCF, 24 Sep 2026 release) and showed 1.6T production test and 3.2T research at ECOC (21–23 Sep). Both support the standards-leadership thesis.
  • Moat: installed base, software and application libraries, a certification role with standards bodies, and 12+ years' average employee tenure (10-K). The risk is cyclicality: FY24 revenue fell 9% when R&D/capex spending paused.

7. Bull / Bear

Bull

  • Orders above $2B for two straight quarters (+56% YoY) with book-to-bill above 1.1, and supplier commitments up to $704M from $450M "to support fulfillment of a strong order backlog" (10-Q).
  • Accelerating revenue (+23% → +32% → +37%) and non-GAAP EPS (+69–79%), with a history of beating its own guide.
  • Margin expansion: segment operating margin rose from about 25% to about 33% in a year.
  • Cash generation (TTM FCF $1.47B) and buybacks ($517M bought in 9 months, $983M left on the $1.5B authorisation). Leverage is moderate (Debt/EBITDA 1.55x).
  • Forward P/E about 27 is cheaper than TER (about 41), with EPS growing about 60%.

Bear

  • Test spending is cyclical. Current strength rides on the AI data-center and defense capex cycle, and a digestion phase would hit orders first.
  • Part of the growth is acquired (Spirent, about $61M a quarter), and the jump in amortisation depresses GAAP numbers.
  • FY26 margins are flattered by the one-off $100M tariff refund, and the tariff/Section 301 regime is still in flux.
  • Weakest technical name on the list (RS stand-in +35). The cup has no handle and the right side is steep (see report).
  • Tax risk: the $107M GILTI refund case won partial summary judgment on 2 Jul 2026, but the government can still appeal. A loss would reverse the recorded benefit (10-Q).

What would change the view: Q4 orders dropping below about $1.9B (book-to-bill under 1), or a guide implying a sharp slowdown into FY27. An adverse tariff or export-control change hitting APAC (44% of revenue). On the positive side, a Q4 beat with FY27 commentary pointing to organic growth above 20%, plus a proper handle forming on volume.

9. Fundamental grade (SEPA lens)

Strong. Revenue has accelerated for three quarters (+23% → +32% → +37%, about +32% excluding Spirent), non-GAAP EPS is up about 70–80%, margins are expanding, orders are running about 1.1–1.2x revenue, and there have been two big beats against guidance. The caveats are the partly acquired growth, a one-off tariff refund in FY26 margins, and a cyclical end market. The fundamentals are ahead of the chart: the technical setup (cup with no handle, weak RS) is the constraint, not the numbers. Research only, not advice.
