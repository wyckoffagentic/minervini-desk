1. Business model and revenue mix

Digital musculoskeletal (MSK) care: AI-powered exercise therapy plus connected hardware (Enso device, motion tracking) plus a remote clinician network. It sells to self-insured employers and health plans, is paid mostly per enrolled member, and uses a fee-for-outcome/performance model (the 10-K refers to annual "yield"). It is expanding into multiple conditions: HingeSelect provider network, Migraine Care, and GI care via the Cylinder Health acquisition ($105M cash, expected to close Q3'26, GI program launching 2027).

  • Channel concentration (10-Q, 1H26): 85% of revenue comes via partners (health plans/PBMs). HCSC 15%, Elevance 13%, Aetna 10%. None of these partners is exclusive.
  • Scale KPIs (Q2'26): 2,929 clients (+24% YoY). LTM billings $861.8M (+52%). Members were 782,890 at YE25 (+47%). Contracted lives were 25M at YE25.
  • A segment or geographic split is not meaningful: the business is US-only with a single segment.

6. Competitive position / moat

  • It is the category leader in digital MSK, with 2,929 clients and deep distribution through the three largest health-plan partners. Health-plan and PBM integration creates a distribution moat that is hard to replicate.
  • Its hardware-plus-AI model lowers the cost per member, as the 86% gross margin shows. Outcome-based pricing and a broadening condition set (migraine, GI) raise wallet share and stickiness.
  • Competitors: Omada, Sword Health (private), and point solutions from payers and PBMs.

7. Bull / Bear

Bull

  • 50%-ish growth with GAAP profitability, 47% FCF margin in Q2, and margins expanding every quarter.
  • A serial beat-and-raise pattern: FY26 revenue guidance was lifted about 16% at the midpoint since February.
  • Multi-condition expansion (migraine, GI) extends the growth runway. Clients +24%.
  • Net cash, and buybacks (~$196.5M done, $300M more authorized) are cutting diluted shares (84.5M in Q4'25 to 83.4M in Q2'26).

Bear

  • Heavy, sustained insider and VC selling at $85–95 (see Risks). Supply overhang.
  • Channel concentration: 85% of revenue via partners, with the top three at 38%. Losing one plan would hurt.
  • Premium valuation (10x EV/Sales, fwd P/E about 37) leaves no room for a slowdown. The Q3 guide implies about +45%.
  • Employer and payer budget pressure, plus risk that GLP-1s change MSK demand [speculative].
  • Integration risk from the Cylinder acquisition. Small/mid-cap in a weak small-cap tape.

What would change the view: A Q3 print without a raise, or growth well below +45%. Loss or renegotiation of a top-3 health-plan partner. Insider selling accelerating further, or a secondary offering.
