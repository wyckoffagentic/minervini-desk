1. Business model and revenue mix

Drug-delivery platform licensor. ENHANZE (rHuPH20 enzyme) lets partners turn IV biologics into subcutaneous (SC) injections. Halozyme earns royalties plus milestones and bulk API sales. Also sells proprietary products (Hylenex, XYOSTED) and partnered auto-injector devices. Hypercon and Surf Bio hyperconcentration technology was added via acquisitions in Nov–Dec 2025.

6. Competitive position / moat

  • ENHANZE is commercially validated: 10 commercial products in 100+ markets. Partners include Roche, J&J, Takeda, Pfizer, BMS, argenx, AbbVie, Lilly, GSK and Incyte. Five new ENHANZE/Hypercon deals were signed YTD by Aug, beating the goal of 3 for the year.
  • Once a drug launches with ENHANZE, switching costs are high because the SC formulation is part of the approved product. Co-formulations can also extend a partner drug's exclusivity beyond the IV patent.
  • Patent cliff: the core rHuPH20 US patent expires in 2027 and the EU patent in 2029, after which royalty rates step down under the contracts. The MDASE (modified hyaluronidase) patents run to 2032–2034. Hypercon and Surf Bio are the next-generation hedge.

7. Bull / Bear

Bull

  • Royalties growing about 50% and still accelerating on three blockbusters plus new launches (Opdivo Qvantig, Ocrevus SC, Rybrevant SC).
  • The guide was raised and consensus sits below it. A beat-and-raise cycle could continue on 2 Nov.
  • High-margin, cash-generative model: about $806M TTM FCF. Buybacks cut the share count 4.2% YoY ($333M repurchased in Q2 at $69.30).
  • Forward P/E of about 11.6 with EPS guided to double. Five new deals YTD extend the pipeline.
  • Upside optionality from the Merck lawsuit over SC Keytruda (MDASE).

Bear

  • The 2027 US / 2029 EU rHuPH20 expiries cut royalty rates. FY27 consensus already shows growth slowing to about +11% revenue.
  • Heavy concentration: the top partner is 41% of revenue and the top 3 are 74% (FY25).
  • Leverage keeps rising: about $2.18B debt at 30 Jun, then $1.5B of new converts. Stockholders' equity is only $144M.
  • Short interest is about 13% of float and the analyst average target of $99.56 is below the price.
  • Litigation cost is rising and the Merck outcome is uncertain.

What would change the view: A guide cut or a royalty miss on 2 Nov. Evidence of partners delaying SC launches. An adverse Merck/MDASE ruling. More convertible issuance or a large cash acquisition. On the positive side, an early FY27 outlook that shows the patent step-down is manageable.

Other notes still outstanding: 2031 0% ($750M issued Nov 2025) and 2032 0.875% ($750M issued Nov 2025), plus the remaining 2027/2028 notes. Settlement is cash and/or shares at the company's election, so dilution depends on the stock staying above the conversion prices. Principal outstanding after the deal is about $3.3B [my estimate from filed amounts; verify in the Q3 10-Q].

  • IP: core patent expiry in 2027/2029, and the Merck patent suit is ongoing, with rising IP litigation cost ($6.9M in Q2).

9. Fundamental grade (SEPA lens)

Strong. Sales and EPS growth are accelerating (+42–52% revenue, non-GAAP EPS +44–48%), margins are high (about 57% operating, 48% FCF), and guidance was raised above consensus, which supports the flat-base breakout. The convert overhang and the 2027 patent step-down are the main fundamental caveats. Research only, not advice.
