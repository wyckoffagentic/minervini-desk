🔎 The lenses

Every setup in the scan can be read through four tribute lenses. Each one is inspired by the published method of a market technician and writes a short 2-3 sentence read in its own style, on the dashboard and in the email, under a header like Weis lens (a tribute to David Weis's method): ….

The lenses are tributes, not impersonations: they never speak as the men, never invent quotes or stories, and every read says what would prove it wrong. The avatars are symbols of each method, not pictures of anyone.

Wyckoff lens

Judges the stock by its own action: supply and demand, effort and result, cause and effect.

A tribute lens inspired by the published work of Richard D. Wyckoff (1873-1934). It is not Mr Wyckoff and is not affiliated with any Wyckoff organisation.

What this lens looks for

  • Position in the campaign: accumulation, markup, distribution or markdown.
  • Whether supply is being absorbed, and whether effort matches result.
  • The cause built in the range, for a point-and-figure count.
  • Critical points: springs, tests and signs of strength.
  • Strength against the general market.

About the inspiration

Pictured in a 1925 portrait as a clean-shaven, bald-domed New York editor in rimless spectacles, a light suit and a high collar. On the page he is formal, orderly and fond of plain analogies. His one rule for his readers was to trust the tape over the tip.

Sources
  • 1925 frontispiece portrait, How I Trade and Invest in Stocks and Bonds (archive.org, public domain)
  • Later portrait (Wikimedia Commons)
  • Wikipedia biography
Sample read (made-up ticker XYZ)

Wyckoff lens (a tribute to Richard D. Wyckoff's method): Judged by its own action, XYZ has spent eleven weeks absorbing supply beneath 42, and the latest dip below the range drew little volume and recovered at once: a spring of the quieter kind. Effort and result now favour demand, and the cause built across the range points toward 50-54. A close back under 39.80 would show the large interests were not ready, and the idea should be abandoned.

Williams lens (VSA)

Reads each bar's spread, close and volume against the background, and asks what the professional money is doing.

A tribute lens inspired by the published work of Tom Williams (1929-2016), originator of Volume Spread Analysis. It is not Mr Williams and is not affiliated with or endorsed by TradeGuider.

What this lens looks for

  • The background: has strength or weakness already shown?
  • Strength on down bars: stopping volume, shakeout, test, no supply.
  • Weakness on up bars: no demand, upthrust, end of a rising market.
  • Spread and close position against volume.
  • Confirmation on the next bar.

About the inspiration

Remembered in his late seventies and eighties as a broad, genial Englishman with a full mane of swept-back grey hair, no glasses, an open-necked shirt and usually no tie. At the lectern he was blunt, animated and hands-up. In interviews he liked to demonstrate the steel ruler he once used to draw charts by hand.

  • Home interview, 2011 (YouTube, Martin Wong)
Sample read (made-up ticker XYZ)

Williams lens (a tribute to Tom Williams's method): Background first: XYZ showed stopping volume at the March low, so strength is already on the chart. Today's down bar came on a narrow spread, closed in the middle and had the lowest volume in two weeks. That's a test, and professional money isn't interested in lower prices. Very, very constructive, provided the next up bar comes on rising volume. A wide-spread down close under 31.20 would cancel the read.

Weis lens (waves)

Reads the chart wave by wave: lines first, then shortening thrusts, wave volume and changes in behaviour.

A tribute lens inspired by the published work of David H. Weis (1942-2020), author of Trades About to Happen. It is not Mr Weis and is not affiliated with or endorsed by his estate or Wyckoff Analytics. Weis Wave® is a registered mark of its owner.

What this lens looks for

  • Lines first: support, resistance and the channel.
  • Wave volume and wave length against the earlier waves.
  • Shortening thrusts, ease of movement against labour, effort against reward.
  • Springs, upthrusts and the springboard (a quiet, narrow, mid-close pullback).
  • Changes in behaviour.

About the inspiration

Remembered in his seventies as a lean, white-bearded former English-literature teacher from Memphis, in round glasses, a navy blazer and an open-collared striped shirt. He was modest, dry-humoured and endlessly patient. He taught by asking students what story the chart was telling, and his talks were full of invitations to look again.

Sources
  • Wyckoff Analytics memorial page
  • Webinar, 'David Weis on Wyckoff, Support/Resistance, and Waves', 2013 (YouTube, futures io)
  • Obituary (Costello Funeral Home)
Sample read (made-up ticker XYZ)

Weis lens (a tribute to David Weis's method): Look at the pullback waves since the breakout: 1.9M, then 1.1M, then 0.4M shares, each shallower than the last, while the up-waves keep lengthening. What's the story here? Supply is running dry and price is resting on the springboard at 58. I'd lean bullish, and a heavy down-wave that closes under 56.50 would be the change in behaviour that changes my mind.

Pruden lens

Treats every chart as a case study: cycle position, the nine tests, the P&F count and 3-to-1 reward/risk.

A tribute lens inspired by the published work of Dr Henry O. "Hank" Pruden (1936-2017), who taught Wyckoff in the graduate classroom. It is not Dr Pruden and is not affiliated with or endorsed by Golden Gate University or any association.

What this lens looks for

  • Cycle position and crowd mood: fear or greed.
  • The Wyckoff phase and the nine buying (or re-accumulation) tests, passed or missing.
  • Spring type #1, #2 or #3, and whether it has been tested.
  • The P&F count target, and reward/risk of at least 3:1.
  • A clear invalidation.

About the inspiration

Remembered as a smiling, white-bearded professor from California in a dark suit and a striped tie, courteous and warm. He welcomed every question and then walked through the case step by step, with a quiet "aha" when the evidence lined up.

Sample read (made-up ticker XYZ)

Pruden lens (a tribute to Hank Pruden's method): Position first: XYZ is late in Phase C of an accumulation, with the crowd still fearful after the 30% decline. Of the nine buying tests, seven are now met; the stock has yet to show higher tops and to outperform the market. Aha, the low-volume test of the #2 spring completes the setup. The P&F count across the base projects 64-71 against a stop under 47, better than 3:1, so the charts suggest a disciplined starter position.
