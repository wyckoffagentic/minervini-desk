THE METHOD verified rules only, with sources

Setups

  • Breakout: a big move (30–100%+) in the past 1–3 months. Then an orderly pullback with higher lows and a tightening range, usually 2 weeks to 2 months, surfing the rising 10/20-day MA. Then a range expansion. [1]
  • Episodic pivot: unexpected news, mostly earnings or guidance, gaps the stock 10%+ on big volume. Best when the stock had not already rallied for 3–6 months. [1]
  • To find leaders, scan for the top 1–2% gainers over 1, 3 and 6 months. [1]

Entry & stop

  • Buy the opening-range high (first 1-, 5- or 60-minute candle) as the stock breaks out. [1]
  • Stop = low of the day, never wider than the stock's ADR/ATR. [1]
  • On this page, Trigger = the range high (EP: the high since the gap). The plan stop is the tighter of the last session's low and Trigger − 1 ADR. Once live, the stop is the breakout day's low.

Sizing

  • Risk 0.25–1% of the account per trade, rarely more than 1%. [1]
  • Most positions are 10–20% of the account. Never hold more than 30% in one stock overnight. [1]

Selling

  • Sell 1/3–1/2 after 3–5 days and move the stop to break-even. [1]
  • Trail the rest on the 10-day MA (fast stocks, and beginners) or the 20-day. Exit on the first close below it. [1] [3]

Market

  • Read the Nasdaq trend from the 10- vs 20-day MA and their slopes. Both rising is good. One turning down means caution. The 10 crossing under the 20 is "not really good". [2]
  • In down-trending markets breakouts don't work well. Trade less and smaller, or sit in cash. [3]
